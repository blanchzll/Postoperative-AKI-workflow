# Clinical supplement eResults (analysis handoff; post hoc)

Under the primary assumed equal-cost budget, the existing selective acquisition rule versus expanded base review changed captured events per 1,000 by −1.73 (95% CI, −6.88 to 4.32) geographically and 1.19 (−1.19 to 3.57) temporally for 9→27 inputs. Corresponding 27→38-input differences were 0.86 (−1.73 to 2.60) and 0.00 (−2.97 to 2.97). No stable advantage supported a mandatory acquisition strategy. Random allocation variation and unfavorable full-acquisition scenarios were retained. These comparisons estimate retrospective score routing at assumed costs, not individual clinical benefit.

The existing individual gate requested updates for 207/1,158 geographic paired records and yielded 480 final reviews capturing 41/72 events at its frozen threshold. No update produced 508 reviews/43 captures; all updates produced 81 reviews/24 captures at that model’s different development-defined threshold. These threshold-burden results are distinct from the equal-budget comparison and cannot select a clinical trigger from the evaluation data.

Actual operation dates permitted study-cohort demand summaries. For geographic center 3 paired records, 438 potential final reviews occurred across 690 active study surgery days (mean 0.635; 95th percentile 2; maximum 3). These are not whole-hospital daily arrivals, completed work or shift workload. Center 5 geographic evaluation contained only 175 records/6 events; temporal center 5 contained 19/0. Fixed-model calibration and eligibility coverage varied by center, with no new blind leave-one-center-out validation.

MIMIC exact-clock replay identified 148 records with measured timed SCr components, sharing 55 records with the frozen 59-event calendar-ratio target; 12 had a measured component before procedure day 0. These definitions were not substituted for one another. The existing INSPIRE recorded surgery-start-to-criterion interval (median 42.92 hours) lacked complete prediction/result-release timing and is not clinical warning lead time.

The geographic 9-input score discriminated 15 recorded 90-day deaths among 1,159 records (AUROC 0.843; 95% CI, 0.752–0.925); the temporal cohort had only 3 recorded deaths among 1,646 evaluable records. Rare-outcome, follow-up and confounding limitations preclude implementation-benefit claims. The temporal 9-input score had readmission AUROC 0.422 (95% CI, 0.382–0.462), versus 0.520 (0.480–0.562) for 27 inputs. Readmission and LOS associations were heterogeneous; all directions remain in the tables.

Geographic preoperative low-risk groups retained 10/335 events with 9 inputs and 24/647 with 27 inputs. In MIMIC, keeping only each day’s first postoperative SCr measurement deleted 522/3,586 measurements (14.6%) and lost 7/59 known events (11.9%; patient-bootstrap 95% CI, 4.3%–20.4%). The geographic six-input low-risk group contained 11/258 events; no temporal six-input record fell below its frozen low-risk threshold. These results do not establish that monitoring can safely stop.

The reverified MIMIC calibrated Brier differences versus the observed target-rate constant were 0.00880 (95% CI, 0.00489–0.01290) geographically and 0.00266 (−0.00012–0.00538) temporally. Original-grid joint bands were verified only within their declared primary comparison/budget family; fixed-choice stability refits and fixed-family CV do not constitute complete selection-optimism correction.
