# Clinical supplement eMethods (analysis handoff; post hoc)

We retained the corrected source data, fitted models, feature sets and development-defined thresholds. Geographic and temporal evaluation records were previously exposed; the new analyses are exploratory rather than fresh blind validation. The 9-to-27-input comparison used the preoperative cohort; the 27-to-38-input comparison used its separately fitted paired cohort. Comparisons concern locked prediction systems, not an isolated causal effect of extra fields: paired geographic routes were Voting versus LR, and temporal paired routes were SGBT versus Voting. The two preoperative comparisons retained LR/LR and Voting/Voting families, respectively. Geographic preoperative/paired evaluation comprised 1,159/1,158 operation records from 1,149/1,148 distinct patient groups; temporal evaluation comprised 1,683/1,011 records from 1,681/1,009 groups. Event counts and capture rates use operation records; uncertainty resamples patient groups.

For information acquisition, the primary budget equalled the cost of reviewing 20% of records plus acquiring additional fields for 25%, with acquisition costing 0.1 review equivalents per record. Integer capacities and unspent fractions were retained. Existing selective, highest-base-risk, nearest-development-cutoff, seeded random, no-acquisition/base-expansion and full-acquisition strategies were compared. Cost ratios of 0, 0.05 and 0.25 were sensitivity scenarios. Acquisition and review were rerouted in 2,000 paired patient-group bootstrap samples (seed 20261108, context offsets); intervals condition on fixed fitted models. An additional 200-seed randomization distribution describes allocation variation, separately from patient uncertainty. Model risk or SHAP values did not define an indication for diagnostic testing.

Frozen per-patient thresholds were used to describe final review demand indexed to actual study-cohort operation dates. Calendar days and active operation days were separate denominators; seven-day moving blocks assessed uncertainty in daily summaries. Counts do not establish actual alert arrival, completed actions, staffing time or complete hospital throughput. Collection and clinical review were costed separately in unmeasured time scenarios. Center-specific calibration, observed-to-expected ratios, review burden and source eligibility coverage were estimated without local adaptation; zero-event strata were qualified.

Chinese POD1/POD2-or-3 windows and MIMIC calendar observations were audited separately. Exact decimal MIMIC measurements were used to identify recorded absolute rises within 48 hours and relative rises against measurements in the preceding seven days, without changing the frozen 59-event target. Source-defined low-risk groups and measurement/window deletions quantified losses of known recorded events. Unknown counterfactual events and monitoring safety were not identified. KDIGO SCr and urine components were distinguished ([KDIGO](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4089654/)).

Frozen AKI scores were related descriptively to recorded hard outcomes and length of stay. RRT contributes to the original recorded AKI definition, so its association is not an independent endpoint validation. Binary-outcome discrimination used patient-group intervals; unadjusted odds ratios per doubling of predicted AKI risk used cluster-robust covariance. These scores were not calibrated mortality probabilities. Index-admission INSPIRE deaths required timestamps between operation end and discharge; later institutional deaths were retained outside that endpoint. No implementation effect was estimated. Alternative numeric implementations independently checked counts, resampling, timed criteria, rank statistics and regression covariance; this was Codex review, not independent human adjudication.
